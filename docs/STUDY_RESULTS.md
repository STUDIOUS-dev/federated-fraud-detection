**2 attackers (bank_c, bank_d) of 4**

| Attack | FedAvg | FedMedian | FedTrimmedAvg | Krum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scale | 0.004 ± 0.001 | 0.003 ± 0.000 | 0.003 ± 0.000 | 0.289 ± 0.072 |
| signflip | 0.003 ± 0.001 | 0.003 ± 0.001 | 0.003 ± 0.001 | 0.289 ± 0.072 |

Reference without any attacker: FedAvg 0.300 ± 0.069, FedMedian 0.299 ± 0.070, FedTrimmedAvg 0.299 ± 0.070, Krum 0.297 ± 0.071.

Attacks: `scale` = send weights x -10; `signflip` = send global + (-5)(local - global); `labelflip` = train on inverted fraud labels. FedTrimmedAvg trims 1 of 4 clients per side (beta=0.25); Krum keeps 1 client.

**Caveats.** (1) Krum returns a single client's model (no averaging), so its robustness is bought with the loss of any federation gain. (2) Krum's guarantee needs n > 2f+2; with n=4, f=2 it is outside its theory. Here it survives only because the two attackers are non-adaptive and far from every honest client; two colluding or mimicking attackers would very likely defeat it. (3) Median and trimmed mean are expected to break at 50% attackers, and they do. (4) `signflip` and `labelflip` barely hurt FedAvg at these settings (local updates are small: lr=0.001, 2 epochs, 5 rounds), so they are weak attacks, not evidence of resilience. (5) FedAvg under the `scale` attack is bimodal across seeds (hence the large std).
